Fire Compliance in South Africa

What does fire compliance mean for your business?

South African businesses have responsibilities under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHS Act) and SANS 10105 to maintain fire equipment and ensure premises are safe. Municipal by-laws may impose additional requirements depending on your building type and occupancy. Non-compliance can affect your insurance position and expose staff and the public to risk.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions.

How often must fire extinguishers be serviced? +
Fire extinguishers must be inspected annually in accordance with SANS 10105. Some units may require more frequent checks depending on their condition, environment or manufacturer recommendations.
What is a Certificate of Compliance? +
A Certificate of Compliance (COC) is a document confirming that fire equipment at a premises has been inspected and that the inspection results meet the required standard. It is typically required by insurers, landlords, and municipal authorities.
Do I need a fire risk assessment? +
Requirements vary depending on your occupancy type, premises size and applicable local authority by-laws. The OHS Act requires employers to identify and mitigate workplace hazards, which includes fire risk. Contact SmartBiz Fire for guidance specific to your premises.
What happens if my business is not compliant? +
Non-compliance can result in issues with your insurance claim validity, local authority inspections and potential liability in the event of an incident. Maintaining up-to-date records and certificates reduces these risks.
