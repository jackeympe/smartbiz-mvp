Why does fire protection maintenance matter?

Fire protection equipment needs to be appropriate for the hazard, accessible and maintained in an operational condition. A documented maintenance programme helps a business identify defects and outstanding actions before an emergency or inspection.

Maintenance should not be reduced to checking whether an extinguisher is mounted on a wall. The condition, suitability, accessibility, service history and required corrective work all matter.

What standards and compliance requirements apply in South Africa?

Fire protection requirements depend on the equipment, property and applicable authority or standard. South African procurement specifications commonly reference SANS standards for firefighting equipment, including SANS 1475 for relevant firefighting equipment production or reconditioning activities.

Businesses should verify the current applicable standard and edition for their specific equipment and service requirement. SmartBiz Fire should publish only credentials, registrations and technical claims that can be independently substantiated.

How often should fire protection equipment be serviced?

There is no single service interval that applies to every fire protection asset. Frequency depends on equipment type, applicable requirements, manufacturer instructions, environment and risk.

A practical programme combines routine visual checks with scheduled professional servicing, defect reporting, corrective maintenance and future service scheduling. Higher-risk environments may need additional inspections.

What businesses need fire protection services?

Almost every occupied commercial or institutional property has fire-safety responsibilities, but the actual equipment and controls should reflect the property's hazards and use.

Offices

Consider electrical equipment, kitchens, server rooms, combustible furnishings, emergency exits and occupancy.

Restaurants

Cooking equipment, heat sources, electrical systems and fuel or grease hazards require appropriate controls and equipment.

Warehouses

Storage density, packaging, combustible stock, chemicals and access routes can materially change fire protection requirements.

Schools

Fire protection planning should consider classrooms, kitchens, laboratories, evacuation routes, staff and learners.

Factories and workshops

Machinery, welding, fuels, gases, chemicals and other ignition sources can require specialised assessment and controls.

Retail premises

Retail businesses should consider stock, electrical equipment, storage areas, customer occupancy and emergency access.

How should you choose a fire protection company?

Choose a provider using verifiable technical credentials, service scope, documentation and operational capability rather than price alone.

  • Confirm relevant company and technician credentials.
  • Ask which standards apply to the equipment being serviced.
  • Check exactly what is included in the quotation.
  • Ask what service documentation will be supplied.
  • Confirm how defects and replacement requirements are reported.
  • For multiple branches, ask whether equipment and service history can be managed centrally.
