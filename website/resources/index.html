Fire Extinguisher Types

Different fire classes require different extinguisher types. Class A fires (wood, paper, fabric) are addressed with water or foam. Class B (flammable liquids) requires CO₂ or dry powder. Class C (gas) needs dry powder or CO₂. Class F (cooking oils) requires wet chemical agents. Using the wrong class of extinguisher on a fire can be ineffective or dangerous.

Annual Fire Safety Checklist

A practical annual checklist for business owners includes: confirm extinguishers are serviced and tagged, check hose reels operate freely, test smoke and heat detectors, verify emergency lighting activates on power loss, check exit signage is illuminated and unobstructed, confirm evacuation plan is posted, and verify fire risk assessment is current.

Understanding Your COC

A Certificate of Compliance (COC) confirms that fire equipment at a premises has been inspected and meets the required standard at the time of inspection. It is commonly required by insurers, commercial landlords and local authorities. A COC has an expiry date — typically linked to the next required inspection — and must be renewed through a follow-up inspection.

Fire Risk Assessment Guide

A fire risk assessment identifies potential ignition sources, fuel loads and people at risk in your premises. It evaluates existing fire prevention and detection measures, identifies gaps, and produces a record of findings with recommended actions. Under the Occupational Health and Safety Act, employers must identify and manage workplace hazards, which includes fire risk. Requirements vary by occupancy type and local authority.

Extinguisher Placement Guide

Extinguishers must be visible, accessible and positioned to allow safe evacuation. SANS 10105 provides guidance on travel distances — typically a maximum of 15 metres between extinguishers in a general commercial environment. Extinguishers should be mounted at the correct height, close to potential ignition sources, and not blocked by furniture, equipment or stock. High-risk areas require dedicated units by type.

Emergency Evacuation Planning

An evacuation plan identifies primary and secondary escape routes, assembly points, warden responsibilities and procedures for assisting people with disabilities. Plans should be posted at building entrances, near lifts and in common areas. Regular evacuation drills — at least annually — help ensure staff can evacuate safely under pressure. The plan should be reviewed whenever the building layout, occupancy or staff changes.
